Which seismic attributes
should you actually use?

A guided attribute selection tool for multi-attribute seismic interpretation and ML workflows

Walk into any seismic interpretation project and you will find one of two problems. Either someone has computed three attributes — envelope, coherence, and curvature — because those are the ones they always compute. Or someone has computed forty-seven attributes because the software let them, and now they are trying to figure out what to do with all of them.

Neither approach is thoughtful. The right attribute set for mapping deepwater channels is not the same as the right set for carbonate reef detection or basement fault mapping. Your data quality, your target depth, whether you have pre-stack data, and whether you are feeding the result into machine learning — all of these change what you should compute.

"Start with the geology. Let the geology tell you which physical properties matter. Then find the attributes that measure those properties."

- the right order of operations

This tool was developed at AASPI (Attribute Assisted Seismic Processing and Interpretation) at the University of Oklahoma. It asks you three sets of questions — about your geologic target, your data, and your workflow — and recommends a tiered starting attribute set with plain-language explanations of what each attribute measures and why it is relevant to your situation.

The recommendations work with any seismic interpretation software. Where AASPI has specific programs that implement an attribute, those are noted. AASPI attributes that are not widely available elsewhere are flagged separately — because knowing they exist is the first step to knowing what you are missing.
